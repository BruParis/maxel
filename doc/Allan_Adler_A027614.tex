\centerline{\bf Appendix 1}\par
Two formulae of Clebsch-Gordan are:

\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
		r_x^m s_y^n                 & = \sum \frac{\binom{m}{k} \binom{n}{k}}{\binom{m+n-k+1}{k}}
		(\overline{rs})^k_{y^{n-k}} (xy)^k,                                                       \\
		(\overline{rs})^k_{y^{n-k}} & = \sum (-1)^k
		\frac{\binom{m}{k} \binom{n}{k}}{\binom{m+n}{k}}
		(rs)^\nu r_x^{m-\nu} s_y^{n-\nu} (xy)^\nu.
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

I prefer to formulate it in terms of matrices. Assume $m\leq n$.
Let $A=(a_{ij})$, with $0\leq i,j\leq m$, where
$$a_{ij}=\cases{
		0 & if $i>j$,\cr
		1 & if $i=j$,\cr
		             {{m-i\choose j-i}{n-i\choose j-i}\over{m+n-i-j+1\choose j-i}} & if
		$i<j$.\cr}$$
Similarly, let $B=(b_{ij})$, with the same range of indices, where
$$b_{ij}=\cases{
	0&if $i>j$,\cr
	1& if $i=j$,\cr
	(-1)^{i+j}{{m-i\choose j-i}{n-i\choose j-i}\over{m+n-2i\choose j-i}}&if
	$i<j$.\cr}$$

Then Gordan's inversion formula amounts to the non-obvious assertion that
$B$ is the inverse of $A$.

I want to record one observation I have made about Gordan's
coefficients. The matrix $A$ is an upper triangular unipotent matrix.
Let $C=(c_{ij})$ be the logarithm of $A$. Then for $j>i$ we have
$$C_{ij}=\kappa_{j-i}A_{ij}\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-2}{1\over n+m-2i-k},$$
where $\kappa=\{\kappa_r\}_{r=1,2,3,\dots}$ is a sequence of integers
independent of $m,n$. We present the first 20 terms of the sequence
